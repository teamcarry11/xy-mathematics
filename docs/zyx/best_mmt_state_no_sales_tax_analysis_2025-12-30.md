## Executive Summary

From a Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) perspective, **Oregon emerges as the best state** for working-class people and legal immigrants among the five states without statewide sales tax. While Alaska offers the unique Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD) that functions like a universal basic income, Oregon provides the best combination of:

- **Progressive income tax** (targets high earners, not working-class)
- **No sales tax** (no regressive consumption tax)
- **Immigrant-friendly policies** (sanctuary cities, supportive environment)
- **Strong job opportunities** (agriculture, construction, technology, healthcare)
- **Reasonable cost of living** (better than Alaska, New Hampshire, or Delaware)
- **Lower property taxes** (0.81% vs New Hampshire's 1.27%)

**Key MMT Insight**: States without sales tax eliminate the most regressive form of taxation. Combined with progressive income taxes that target high earners (not working-class people), this creates an optimal MMT-aligned tax structure that maximizes effective demand from working-class spending while managing inflation through taxes on high earners with high savings rates.

- **State Income Tax**: Progressive, 4.75% to 9.9%

- **Property Tax**: 0.81% effective rate (median home: $440,228)

## MMT Analysis: Tax Structure

### Modern Monetary Theory Principles Applied

From an MMT perspective, optimal tax policy for working-class people should:

1. **Eliminate regressive taxes** (sales tax, flat taxes on necessities)
2. **Minimize taxes on working-class people** (they spend all their income → drive effective demand)
3. **Target high earners/wealth holders** (they have high savings rates → can manage inflation through taxing them)
4. **Support effective demand** (working-class spending drives the economy)

### State-by-State MMT Tax Analysis

#### Alaska: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)

**Strengths**:
- ✅ No income tax (working-class keeps all earned income)
- ✅ No sales tax (no regressive consumption tax)
- ✅ **Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD)** — functions like UBI, directly supports working-class people
- ✅ PFD reduces poverty by 20-40% (proven impact)
- ✅ PFD stimulates local economy (15-25% retail sales spike in October)

**Weaknesses**:
- ⚠️ Property tax 1.14% (higher than Oregon, Delaware, Montana)
- ⚠️ PFD sustainability concerns (fund may be depleted in coming years)

**MMT Assessment**: Excellent for working-class people. The PFD is a unique MMT-aligned feature that directly supports effective demand. No income or sales tax means maximum money in working-class hands. However, property tax is higher than optimal, and PFD sustainability is a concern.

**Working-Class Tax Burden** (for $50k income):
- Income tax: $0
- Sales tax: $0
- Property tax (on $395k home): ~$4,504/year
- **Net: PFD provides ~$2,275, offsetting property tax burden**

#### Delaware: ⭐⭐⭐ (3/5)

**Strengths**:
- ✅ No sales tax (no regressive consumption tax)
- ✅ Lowest property tax in nation (0.41%)
- ✅ Progressive income tax (0% to 6.6% — targets high earners)

**Weaknesses**:
- ⚠️ Income tax still applies to working-class (though progressive, still reduces effective demand)
- ⚠️ No special programs like Alaska's PFD

**MMT Assessment**: Good, but not optimal. Progressive income tax is better than flat tax, but still taxes working-class people. No sales tax is excellent. Lowest property tax is a major advantage. However, income tax on working-class reduces effective demand compared to Alaska.

**Working-Class Tax Burden** (for $50k income):
- Income tax: ~$1,200-1,500 (estimated, depends on brackets)
- Sales tax: $0
- Property tax (on $406k home): ~$1,666/year
- **Total: ~$2,866-3,166/year**

#### Montana: ⭐⭐⭐ (3/5)

**Strengths**:
- ✅ No sales tax (no regressive consumption tax)
- ✅ Lowest median home price ($237,357) — most affordable housing
- ✅ Lower property tax (0.83%)

**Weaknesses**:
- ⚠️ Income tax 4.70-5.90% (applies to working-class, reduces effective demand)
- ⚠️ Not progressive enough (flat graduated rate, not truly progressive)
- ⚠️ Less immigrant-friendly policies (bans sanctuary cities)

**MMT Assessment**: Moderate. No sales tax is good, and lowest home prices help. But income tax on working-class reduces effective demand. Less immigrant-friendly policies are a concern.

**Working-Class Tax Burden** (for $50k income):
- Income tax: ~$2,350-2,950/year (4.70-5.90% of $50k)
- Sales tax: $0
- Property tax (on $237k home): ~$1,967/year
- **Total: ~$4,317-4,917/year**

#### New Hampshire: ⭐⭐ (2/5)

**Strengths**:
- ✅ No income tax on earned income (working-class keeps all wages)
- ✅ No sales tax (no regressive consumption tax)
- ✅ Only taxes dividends/interest (targets wealth, not working-class)

**Weaknesses**:
- ❌ Highest property tax of the five (1.27%)
- ❌ Highest median home price ($465,586)

**MMT Assessment**: Mixed. No income tax on earned income is excellent for working-class. But highest property tax and home prices create significant burden. Less immigrant-friendly policies are problematic.

**Working-Class Tax Burden** (for $50k income):
- Income tax: $0 (on earned income)
- Sales tax: $0
- Property tax (on $466k home): ~$5,918/year
- **Total: ~$5,918/year** (highest of the five)

#### Oregon: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)

**Strengths**:
- ✅ No sales tax (no regressive consumption tax)
- ✅ Progressive income tax (4.75% to 9.9% — targets high earners)
- ✅ Lower property tax (0.81%)
- ✅ Immigrant-friendly policies (sanctuary cities, supportive environment)
- ✅ Strong job opportunities (agriculture, construction, tech, healthcare)
- ✅ Reasonable cost of living (better than Alaska, NH, Delaware)

**Weaknesses**:
- ⚠️ Income tax still applies to working-class (though progressive, still reduces effective demand)
- ⚠️ No special programs like Alaska's PFD

**MMT Assessment**: **Best overall**. Progressive income tax targets high earners (not working-class as much). No sales tax eliminates regressive taxation. Lower property tax than Alaska or New Hampshire. Immigrant-friendly policies support integration. Strong job market supports economic participation.

**Working-Class Tax Burden** (for $50k income):
- Income tax: ~$2,375-3,000/year (4.75-6% estimated on $50k)
- Sales tax: $0
- Property tax (on $440k home): ~$3,564/year

**Note**: While Oregon's total tax burden appears higher than Alaska, the combination of progressive income tax (targeting high earners), no sales tax, lower property tax, immigrant-friendly policies, and strong job opportunities makes it the best overall choice.

## Special Factors

### Alaska's Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD)

**What It Is**: Annual payments to all Alaska residents from oil revenue

**2025 Amount**: ~$2,275 per resident

**MMT Impact**:
- ✅ Functions like Universal Basic Income (UBI)
- ✅ Directly supports working-class people
- ✅ Reduces poverty by 20-40%
- ✅ Stimulates local economy (15-25% retail sales spike)
- ✅ No work disincentive (employment-to-population ratio unchanged)
- ✅ Supports effective demand (working-class spending)

**Sustainability Concerns**:
- ⚠️ Permanent Fund reserves may be depleted in coming years
- ⚠️ Dependent on oil revenue volatility

**MMT Assessment**: The PFD is a unique MMT-aligned feature that directly supports working-class people and effective demand. However, sustainability concerns are significant.

### Oregon's Progressive Income Tax

**What It Is**: Progressive tax structure (4.75% to 9.9%) that targets high earners

**MMT Impact**:
- ✅ Targets high earners (who have high savings rates)
- ✅ Minimizes burden on working-class (lower rates for lower incomes)
- ✅ Manages inflation by taxing those who save, not those who spend
- ✅ Supports redistribution

**MMT Assessment**: Optimal MMT-aligned tax structure that targets the right group (high earners with high savings rates) while minimizing burden on working-class people who drive effective demand.

From a Modern Monetary Theory perspective, **Oregon is the best state** for working-class people and legal immigrants among the five states without statewide sales tax. Oregon provides:

1. **Optimal MMT-aligned tax structure**: No sales tax (eliminates regressive taxation) + progressive income tax (targets high earners, not working-class) + lower property tax

**New Hampshire** has no income tax on earned income, but highest property tax and home prices, plus less immigrant-friendly policies, make it the least recommended.

**The MMT Bottom Line**: Oregon's tax structure maximizes effective demand from working-class spending while managing inflation through taxes on high earners (who save), not working-class people (who spend). Combined with immigrant-friendly policies and strong job opportunities, Oregon is the optimal choice for working-class people and legal immigrants.
