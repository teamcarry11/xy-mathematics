# First Principles Product Development: What We Know Works

## The Observable Facts

We observe these facts about products that succeed:

1. **People use products that solve real problems.** This is observable. Products that solve problems get used. Products that don't, don't.

2. **People prefer products that are complete within their scope.** This is testable. A simple product that does its job well is preferred over a complex product that does its job poorly.

3. **People form emotional connections with products that align with their values.** This is measurable. People choose products that reflect who they are and what they believe.

4. **Products that are built with care are more likely to be cared for.** This is observable. Code written with discipline is easier to maintain. Systems designed with intention are easier to extend.

5. **Complexity increases failure modes.** This is testable. More moving parts mean more things that can break. Simpler systems have fewer failure modes.

6. **People can tell when something is built with integrity.** This is observable. They can tell when care was taken, when corners were cut, when the work was done right.

These are not opinions. These are observable facts about how products succeed and fail.

## The First Principles

From these observable facts, we derive first principles:

### Principle 1: Solve Real Problems

We build products that solve real problems for real people. We observe what problems exist. We test whether our solution actually solves them. We measure whether people use it.

This is not about what we think people need. This is about what we observe people actually need.

### Principle 2: Complete Within Scope

We build products that are complete within their scope. A simple product that does its job completely is better than a complex product that does its job incompletely.

We define scope clearly. We build to that scope completely. We do not build beyond scope until the scope is complete.

This is not about feature lists. This is about completeness within defined boundaries.

### Principle 3: Align With Values

We build products that align with the values of the people we serve. We observe what they value. We build products that reflect those values.

For Grain OS, we observe that the people we serve value:
- Spiritual warmheartedness
- Detachment from broken systems
- Rejection of paths with repugnant conflicts of interest
- Systems thinking
- Patient discipline
- Integrity over expedience

We build products that reflect these values. This is not about marketing. This is about alignment.

### Principle 4: Build With Care

We build products with care. We write code with discipline. We design systems with intention. We test thoroughly. We document clearly.

This is not about perfectionism. This is about doing the work right the first time, because doing it wrong costs more later.

### Principle 5: Prefer Simplicity

We prefer simplicity over complexity. We choose the simplest solution that solves the problem completely. We remove unnecessary parts. We reduce moving parts.

This is not about avoiding hard problems. This is about solving them simply.

### Principle 6: Build With Integrity

We build products with integrity. We do not cut corners. We do not ship broken things. We do not make promises we cannot keep.

This is observable. People can tell. They know when something is built with integrity, and they respond to it.
